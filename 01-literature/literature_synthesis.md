# Literature Synthesis: Medication Burden, Micronutrients, and Physical Performance

## Overview
Evidence links micronutrient status (vitamin D, folate, B12-related pathways) and medication burden to functional outcomes in older adults. NHANES-based studies show associations between serum vitamin D or folate and functional disability, and homocysteine (a B12/folate-related marker) with gait speed and strength. Polypharmacy is consistently associated with worse physical function, balance, and falls, but interaction studies between medication burden and micronutrient status on performance outcomes remain limited.

## Micronutrient status and performance/balance outcomes
- Vitamin D: Multiple cohort studies (including NHANES and InCHIANTI) report lower 25(OH)D associated with poorer gait speed and lower-extremity performance, with evidence of biomarker interaction (vitamin D x IL-6) for slow gait.
- Folate: NHANES analyses show higher serum folate associated with lower odds of functional disability; non-NHANES cohorts link folate/B12/homocysteine profiles to physical function.
- Vitamin B12: Observational cohorts show B12 and homocysteine associations with gait speed and frailty, but NHANES-specific B12-performance analyses are sparse.

## Medication burden and performance/balance outcomes
- Systematic review evidence links polypharmacy with reduced physical function.
- Cross-sectional studies show polypharmacy associated with poorer balance (BBS, TUG) and higher falls risk.
- Studies of falls in older adults highlight medication count as a mediator of gait and balance risk pathways.

## Implications for the proposed study
- NHANES provides validated micronutrient biomarkers and functional disability outcomes; gait speed and balance measures are available in select cycles and can be paired with prescription counts.
- A combined exposure model (prescription count x vitamin D/B12/folate status) addresses a clear gap in interaction evidence and can clarify whether medication burden modifies micronutrient-performance relationships.

## Gaps to address
- Limited NHANES studies that jointly evaluate medication burden and micronutrient biomarkers against objective physical performance and balance outcomes.
- Balance-specific outcomes are less frequent than gait speed or disability indices in large surveys.
- Few studies explicitly model interactions between polypharmacy and micronutrient status on performance outcomes.
